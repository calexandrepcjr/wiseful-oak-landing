# Embedded Tech Leadership vs. Staff Augmentation

Most companies in friction don't need more hands. They need **clearer ownership**, a **stable core**, and someone willing to make the unpopular call about what to stop doing.

## The staffing trap

Staff augmentation feels safe: predictable hourly rate, plug-and-play resourcing, easy to cancel. But it quietly assumes the hardest problem is throughput. In our experience, the hardest problem is almost always **direction**.

A team of ten contractors building the wrong thing on top of an unstable platform will not ship faster than four senior people fixing the platform first.

## What "embedded" actually means

When we embed, we:

1. Diagnose the failure mode (scaling, cost, instability, ownership) in the first two weeks
2. Stabilize the core systems and the decision-making around them
3. Transition ownership back to the internal team with documented playbooks
4. Stay on as advisors — never as a permanent crutch

## The exit is the product

The strongest signal of a healthy engagement is that our involvement decreases over time. If it doesn't, something is wrong with how we're working — not with the client.
